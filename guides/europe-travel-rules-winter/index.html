Flying to Europe this winter: 4 new rules

Watch the full video on YouTube: Flying to Europe This Winter? 4 New Rules That Could Wreck Your Trip

Flying to Europe from the UK this winter? Four things have changed: the new border check, a travel permit you can't buy yet, a liquids rule that depends on the airport, and a "free" cabin bag that mostly isn't free yet for us. Here's what each one means, where we'd still go, and a checklist. These are the facts as we found them in October 2026; sources are listed at the bottom and in the video description.

Rule 1: EES, the new border check

The EU Entry/Exit System swaps the passport stamp for your photo and fingerprints. It started on 12 October 2025 and ABTA says it went fully live on 10 April 2026. It's free, there's nothing to do before you go, and once you're registered it lasts three years or until you get a new passport.

The catch is the queues. In October, Europe's airports and airlines said waits were still "unacceptably high": up to five hours at peak in the worst cases. Which? Travel said if you miss your flight because of the border queue, your travel insurance probably won't cover it. To be fair, an ABTA survey found half of people queued under 30 minutes and 87% were through in under an hour. It's a lottery.

Rule 2: ETIAS, the £17 permit you can't buy yet

ETIAS will cost €20 (about £17), free for under-18s and over-70s, and last three years. But right now it isn't running and no applications are being taken. ABTA has warned about scam websites already "selling" it. Don't pay anyone for ETIAS. When it opens, only use the EU's own site: travel-europe.europa.eu/etias.

Rule 3: the liquids lottery (100ml or 2 litres)

Some UK airports with new scanners let you take containers up to 2 litres through security (Heathrow, Gatwick, Edinburgh, Birmingham, Bristol and Belfast City). Many others still say 100ml. Liverpool's own help page says you don't need the plastic bag any more, but each bottle must still be 100ml or less. The rule belongs to the airport, including the one you fly home from, so pack for 100ml and you're fine everywhere.

Rule 4: the "free" cabin bag

The EU has provisionally agreed that a standard ticket would include a small personal item and a cabin bag up to 7kg. Euronews said the earliest start is October 2027. easyJet told Which? that for British passengers it would apply only on return flights from EU countries. So for now, still budget for the cabin bag, especially on the way out of the UK.

Where we'd still go this winter

  • Tenerife: Which? found return flights from Liverpool for £31. It's Spain, so EES applies; leave loads of time on the way home.
  • Marrakech, Morocco: not in Schengen, so no EES and no ETIAS. Which? found returns from about £34 from Manchester.
  • Christmas markets: FlightTribe priced Zagreb at £159 each from London for a return flight, half a hotel room for two nights and four mulled wines. From Liverpool, FlightTribe found easyJet to Berlin on Sunday 6 December for £25.99 one way.

Fares are what was found when they were checked, not a promise. Prices move every day.

The 5-point checklist

  1. Coming home from Schengen: allow at least an extra hour (more at the big Spanish airports) and avoid tight connections.
  2. Never pay anyone for ETIAS. It isn't open yet. Only use the EU's own site.
  3. Pack liquids at 100ml. It works at every airport.
  4. Still budget for the cabin bag, especially on the way out of the UK.
  5. Want winter sun with no new border system? Look at Morocco.
